POS Ecosystem Basics

Understand the full system before selling individual devices.

⏱ 5 min

What is a POS system?

Point of Sale is where orders, payments, receipts, kitchen routing, reporting, and customer data come together.

For restaurants, the POS is not just a cash register. It is the operational hub that connects front counter, servers, kitchen, payment devices, online ordering, loyalty, and reporting.

Typical restaurant POS flow

1. OrderTerminal, handheld, kiosk, online
2. RouteKDS or kitchen printer
3. PrepareKitchen completes order
4. PayCash, card, tap, gift card
5. ReportSales, labor, EOD reports
Sales rep takeaway: Always explain POS as a connected ecosystem, not one single device.

POS Terminal

The dedicated drive terminal and operational hub.

⏱ 6 min

Reference image

What it is and how it is used

  • Core of the POS system: main device where cashier or manager operates the restaurant order flow.
  • Order taking: enter dine-in, takeout, delivery, pickup, modifiers, discounts, taxes, and payment type.
  • Receipt printing: sends customer receipt to receipt printer and order chits to kitchen printer when configured.
  • Payment control: connects with payment terminal/card reader for swipe, chip, tap, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and gift card transactions.
  • Table management: manage table status, check status, split checks, move checks, and close checks.
  • Employee login: staff usually log in using PIN/credentials; permissions control what they can access.
  • Shift change: staff can clock in/out, change users, or switch roles based on permissions.
  • Reports: shift report, server report, end-of-day report, sales summary, payment summary, and online order visibility.
  • Peripheral hub: Usually acts as the main connection point for receipt printer, drawer, payment terminal, scanner, and kitchen routing.
  • Operational control: Managers can use it for refunds, discounts, table changes, employee access, and shift closeout.
Advantage: Terminal hardware is designed to run restaurant operations reliably all day and acts as the central hub for connected peripherals.

Handheld POS & Self-Ordering Kiosk

Mobile ordering and customer self-service devices.

⏱ 7 min

Handheld POS / Tablet

What it is: A portable POS device used by servers to take orders and payments directly at the table, patio, bar, or anywhere in the restaurant.
  • Best fit: Fine dining, full-service restaurants, bars, patios, and busy dining rooms where staff move between tables.
  • Order at table: Server enters the order directly into POS instead of writing it on paper and re-entering it later.
  • Better order accuracy: No manual lecture/waiter writing errors; the order is captured in POS and routed immediately.
  • Table payment: Customer can pay at the table using tap, swipe, chip, Apple Pay, Google Pay, or other supported payment methods.
  • Built-in card read/payment flow: Credit card can be read at the table, reducing the need to carry the card away from the guest.
  • Efficiency: Server no longer needs to walk back to the terminal for every order or payment.
  • Revenue impact: Faster service can increase tips, table turnover, and repeat customer experience.
  • Labor impact: Reduces unnecessary staff movement and helps improve employee retention by making the job easier.

Usual connections

  • Wi-Fi: primary connection to POS, kitchen routing, and cloud sync.
  • Bluetooth: can connect to card reader or mobile printer depending on setup.
  • Cloud sync: order, table, and payment status update in real time.
Sales benefit: The server can take the order, send it to kitchen, and collect payment without leaving the guest — improving speed, accuracy, tips, and customer experience.

Self-Ordering Kiosk

What it is: A customer-facing ordering device that lets guests browse the menu, customize items, apply loyalty/discounts, and pay without cashier assistance.
  • Camera / scan support: Can be used for loyalty, discounts, QR code, coupons, or customer identification flows where enabled.
  • Payment: Customer can complete payment directly at the kiosk.
  • Customer efficiency: Staff can focus on food prep, guest service, and exception handling instead of only taking orders.
  • Visual selling: Product images, modifiers, and add-ons help push guests toward higher-ticket items.
  • Consistent upsell: Kiosk does not forget to ask for combo upgrades, drinks, desserts, sauces, or limited-time offers.
  • Marketing value: Great place to show LTOs, specials, loyalty enrollment, seasonal promos, and high-margin items.
  • Guest control: Customer can review their order before paying, which reduces misunderstanding and order corrections.

Usual connections

  • Ethernet or Wi-Fi: connects to POS, menu, cloud, and order routing.
  • Integrated payment device: supports card/contactless payment depending on setup.
  • Printer/KDS routing: completed orders are sent automatically to kitchen printer or KDS.
Sales benefit: Kiosk improves customer flow, reduces cashier workload, increases upsell consistency, and creates a strong marketing surface for LTOs and specials.

Printers, Label Printer & Cash Drawer

Receipts, kitchen chits, product labels, and secure cash handling.

⏱ 5 min

Thermal Receipt Printer

  • Customer receipt: Prints the final receipt for the guest after payment.
  • Thermal technology: Uses heat-sensitive paper, so there is no ink cartridge to replace.
  • Barcode / QR code: Can print barcode or QR code for loyalty, receipt lookup, customer message, survey, coupon, or digital receipt flow.
  • Common connections: Usually connects through USB, Ethernet/network, or Wi-Fi depending on printer model and store setup.
  • Quantity: Typically one receipt printer per POS terminal at the counter.
  • Reliability: Commercial thermal printers are generally durable, with expected life span around 3–4 years depending on volume and environment.
  • Cash drawer trigger: Often sends the signal to open the cash drawer after a cash transaction.
Sales benefit: Reliable receipt printing keeps checkout moving and supports loyalty/marketing messaging directly on the customer receipt.

Kitchen & Expo Printer

  • Kitchen instructions: Sends clear order instructions to the kitchen so staff know what to prepare.
  • Environmental impact: Kitchen heat, moisture, grease, and steam can affect printer life and reliability.
  • Ticket clarity: Prints item details, modifiers, notes, and customizations such as “No cheese” or special prep instructions for better accuracy.
  • Multi-printer prep station: A restaurant may use multiple printers so each prep station gets only what it needs — for example grill, salad, bar, pizza, or dessert.
  • Expo prep: Expo printer can combine all items into a full ticket so the expo team can validate the complete order before handing it to guest or driver.
  • Delivery support: Helps organize pickup/delivery orders from channels like Uber Eats or other online sources when routed through POS.
  • KDS transition: Many modern stores are replacing kitchen printers with KDS, but printers are still common as backup or for specific stations.
Sales rep note: Explain kitchen printers as station-specific instruction tools. Explain KDS as the more modern digital version with better tracking and visibility.

Label Printer

What it is: A dedicated printer that prints adhesive labels for individual products, prepared items, packaged orders, or expo validation.
Reference label example
Order:#1842
Name:John S.
Type:Takeout
Item:1 of 3
  • Burger — no cheese
  • Salad — dressing side
  • Drink — medium
Validated by Expo • 12:42 PM
  • Product identification: Prints the guest name, order type, item count such as “1 of 3,” product name, and product modifications.
  • Complex order support: Useful when one order has multiple products like burger, salad, drink, sides, and custom modifications.
  • Kitchen accuracy: Kitchen can place the label on the product as the item is being made, helping confirm the right product and right modification.
  • Expo validation: Expo team can review labels to assemble the full order correctly before handoff.
  • Sticker use cases: Commonly used on burgers, platters, pizza boxes, coffee cups, bakery items, takeout bags, and delivery packaging.
  • Order assembly: Helps assemble multiple items into one expo ticket/order by matching name, order number, item count, and preparation notes.
  • Server speed: Makes it faster for servers, runners, or pickup staff to identify the correct order.

Usual connections

  • USB: direct connection to a terminal or station device.
  • Ethernet / Wi-Fi: network printer setup for kitchen or expo station routing.
  • POS routing: Labels print based on menu item, station, or order type configuration.
Sales benefit: Label printers improve product accuracy, speed up expo assembly, and reduce wrong-item handoffs for complex orders.

Cash Drawer

What it is: A secure physical drawer mounted under the counter or cabinet for handling cash transactions during a shift.

  • Connection: Usually connects through the receipt printer using a drawer cable. The POS sends the signal to the printer, and the printer opens the drawer.
  • Purpose: Opens only when cash payment is selected or when a permitted “no sale/open drawer” action is performed.
  • Mounting: Typically mounted under the counter or inside a cabinet for better security and cleaner counter space.
  • Lock: Includes a physical lock/key so cash can be secured if power or printer connection is unavailable.
  • Removable till: Usually includes a plastic till insert that can be removed at shift close and taken to the office or safe for counting.
  • Multiple tills: A store may use several tills that are stored safely and assigned to employees or shifts.
  • Shift process: Employee starts the shift with a till, uses it during transactions, then removes it after logout/shift close for counting.
  • Accountability: Assigning a till to an employee improves cash accountability and helps identify shortages or overages.
  • Reporting: Drawer activity supports end-of-day reconciliation, cash expected vs actual, paid-in/paid-out, and no-sale tracking.
Common troubleshooting: If the drawer does not open, check printer power, printer connection, drawer cable, drawer assignment, and cash drawer settings inside POS.

Kitchen Display System (KDS)

Digital kitchen workflow and order accuracy.

⏱ 5 min

Reference image

What it is and how it is used

  • Digital screen used by kitchen staff to view incoming orders.
  • Replaces or supplements kitchen paper tickets.
  • Orders can be sorted by time, station, priority, or fulfillment type.
  • Kitchen can bump/complete orders when finished.
  • Helps track prep time, late orders, and kitchen bottlenecks.
  • Improves order accuracy because modifiers and notes are visible and organized.
  • Can replace many kitchen/expo printer workflows in modern stores.
  • Expo can use KDS to validate the full order before it is handed to guest, server, or delivery driver.
Sales benefit: No lost tickets, less confusion, faster kitchen communication, and stronger visibility than paper-only printer workflows.

Connection Types & Troubleshooting

How POS devices usually communicate.

⏱ 7 min

Connection types

ConnectionUsually used forBest explanation
USBReceipt printer, label printer, scanner, local peripheralsDirect wired connection; simple and reliable.
Ethernet / LANKDS, kitchen printer, label printer, terminal networkWired network; best for stability.
Wi-FiHandheld, tablet, kiosk, KDSWireless connection; flexible but depends on network quality.
BluetoothCard reader, mobile printer, scannerShort-range wireless connection.
CloudReporting, menu sync, online ordersData syncing and remote access.

Recommended restaurant network setup

1
Stable internet
Use business-grade internet where possible. POS depends on reliable connectivity for cloud sync and online orders.
2
Wired where possible
Use Ethernet for kitchen printers and KDS if the location supports it.
3
Strong Wi-Fi
Handhelds and kiosks need strong Wi-Fi coverage across dining area, counter, patio, and kitchen pass-through.
4
Device naming
Name devices clearly: Front Printer, Kitchen Printer, Expo KDS, Bar KDS, Handheld 1, etc.

Common troubleshooting checklist

  • Device powered on?
  • Connected to correct Wi-Fi or Ethernet?
  • Printer has paper and lid closed?
  • Cash drawer cable connected to printer?
  • Label printer has labels loaded and correct label size?
  • Payment device paired/online?
  • KDS assigned to correct kitchen station?
  • Menu item configured to route to correct printer/KDS?
