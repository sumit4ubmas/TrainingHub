The field mindset: how to approach any competitor conversation

1
Never attack directly
Validate their current choice first. "That's a well-known system for a reason." Owners who feel judged stop listening immediately.
2
Discover the hidden pain
Ask questions that surface what their current system can't do. Every competitor has a gap — your job is to find the one that hurts most.
3
Bridge to Plum's strength
Once you find the pain, bridge directly to Plum's matching advantage. One problem, one solution, one outcome. Don't list features.
4
Close with proof
Name a local restaurant or known brand that made the switch. Social proof closes the deal that logic opened.

Plum vs. Toast

Most common competitor in the field — learn every weakness cold

⏱ 5 min
Toast is your #1 competitor. You will encounter it constantly. Know every weakness, every counter, and every script on this page by heart.

Toast weaknesses vs. Plum advantages

❌ Toast Weaknesses
Terrible supportLimited hours — 7AM to 12AM EST only. Long wait times, slow emergency resolution. If your restaurant goes down at 1AM on a Saturday, you're on your own.
Expensive add-onsCommon features cost extra. Online ordering: $150/mo. Third-party integration: $75/mo. Loyalty program: $50/mo. Monthly subscription: $90/mo. It adds up fast.
Inflexible ratesOne rate option — rates climb as you add features. Next-day deposits cost an additional ~2.5% fee. No flexibility for the merchant.
✅ Plum Advantages
24/7 human supportFriendly, trained staff available around the clock. Fast emergency resolution — included at no extra charge. A restaurant that's down less earns more.
Flexible pricingChoose your payment model. Low equipment costs, no surprise charges. Features are included — not sold back to you one by one.
Better merchant servicesFlexible and consistent rates. Next-day deposits available without the punishing extra fee.

Toast real-world pricing (what they actually pay)

Toast's advertised pricing is deceptively low. The real monthly cost hits $550/mo once you add hardware, devices, and the features every restaurant actually needs.
Equipment (all-in)$185/mo
Per device fee (e.g. 1 additional device)+$50/mo
Online ordering / delivery integration+$150/mo
Third-party integrations+$75/mo
Loyalty program+$50/mo
Software subscription+$90/mo
Realistic total (hardware + subscription + apps)~$550/mo
Processing rates: 1.75% debit + $0.02/swipe  ·  2.9% credit + $0.01/swipe  ·  3.5% + $0.15 keyed & AMEX  ·  Next-day deposits available at ~2.5% fee
Plum's full-featured setup starts at a fraction of this. When you show this math on paper in front of an owner, deals close.

Handling the Toast objection

💬 "We use Toast and it works fine for us"
Owner
"We've been on Toast for two years. It works fine."
Rep — validate + probe support
"Toast is a big name for a reason — totally get it. Quick question: have you ever had to call their support after midnight? Or on a Sunday morning before your brunch rush?"
Owner
"Actually yeah, we had an issue one night and couldn't get anyone for hours."
Rep — bridge to Plum
"That's the one thing we hear constantly from Toast customers. Plum has 24/7 live support — real people, not a ticket queue. And honestly, when you add up what Toast charges for online ordering, loyalty, and integrations on top of the hardware, most owners are paying around $550 a month before a single transaction. I can show you what that looks like on paper and compare it to what Plum costs all-in. Takes two minutes."
Talking Point

"A restaurant that's down less earns more. Lead with uptime and support reliability — then show the real monthly cost comparison on paper."

Plum vs. Clover

Strong in mom-and-pop restaurants — exploit the reseller support gap

⏱ 5 min
Clover's biggest liability is its reseller-dependent support model. Clover doesn't support restaurants directly — they route everything through third-party resellers with variable quality and extra fees.

Clover weaknesses vs. Plum advantages

❌ Clover Weaknesses
Hit-or-miss supportSupport routed through resellers — variable hours, few knowledgeable staff, long resolution times, and extra fees just to get problems fixed.
Deposits cost extraNext-day deposits require an additional 1.75% fee. Card processing options are rigid with little flexibility for the merchant.
Can't handle complexityDesigned for simple operations — modifications and complex ordering workflows can't be handled by Clover's interface. Fine for a coffee shop, not for a full-service restaurant.
✅ Plum Advantages
Staffed help desk, always on24/7 direct phone access to trained support staff. Problems get solved fast — and it's completely free for all Plum users. No reseller in the middle.
Enhanced merchant servicesFlexible card processing — owners choose the rate structure that works for them. Next-day funding available without a punishing surcharge.
Designed for all operationsPlum's intuitive UI handles complex modifications, large parties, split checks, and custom ordering workflows — while still generating extra revenue through upsells.

Plum vs. Square

Popular with small operators — but built for retail, not restaurants

⏱ 4 min
Square has shifted its focus away from POS and is now geared mainly towards card processing — where before they were a significant POS player. Their restaurant features are an afterthought, and owners paying volume-dependent rates are often overpaying significantly as volumes grow.

Square weaknesses vs. Plum advantages

❌ Square Weaknesses
Slow, inconsistent supportLong waits, conflicting guidance, and very hard to reach in emergencies. Not built for the urgency of a restaurant going down mid-service.
Clunky interfaceSlow order entry and limited menu customization reduce table turnover and hourly sales. Staff takes longer — and that time costs money.
Hidden, high card ratesVolume-dependent fees with little transparency. Owners often don't know what they're actually paying until they dig into their statements.
Lack of restaurant featuresSquare's retail roots mean no drive-thru support, no integrated kiosk, limited complex discount management, and no native online ordering.
✅ Plum Advantages
24/7 live supportTrained staff available by phone — resolves issues quickly every time.
Intuitive UIStaff onboard with minimal training. Faster ordering means more customers served per hour — a direct revenue impact.
Transparent, flexible ratesConsistent pricing with clear terms plus next-day deposits for improved cash flow.
Built for restaurantsDrive-thru support, integrated kiosk, complex discount and menu management, handheld POS — all native, not bolted on.
Talking Point

"Plum's speed translates directly to more revenue. The Return on Investment increases when you add in lower card costs."

Fast 24/7 supportTrained support, an intuitive UI, and issues resolved quickly every time. No repeated escalations, no disorganized onboarding.

How to open a legacy system conversation

Opening line for Aloha or Micros owners "I noticed you're on [Aloha/Micros] — you've clearly been in this business a long time. I'm not here to tell you your system is bad. But I talk to a lot of owners on legacy systems who are spending thousands a year on maintenance and training that they could be putting back into the restaurant. Would it be worth 5 minutes to see what's changed in POS technology in the last few years?"
Legacy owners feel loyal to systems they've used for years. Respect that loyalty, then redirect it toward what they're losing — not what they have.

The savings conversation — how to run the math live

1
Ask for their monthly volume
"Roughly what do you process in credit card transactions per month?"
2
Calculate their current processing cost
Multiply their volume by their current rate. Example: $50,000 × 3.5% (Toast) = $1,750/mo in card fees.
3
Calculate Plum's cost
$50,000 × 2.2% + $0.15 × transactions ≈ significantly lower. Show the difference on paper.
4
Add the add-on savings
If they're paying Toast for integrations + loyalty, add another $200+ to the monthly savings column.
5
Close with the annual number
"That's [monthly savings] × 12 = [annual savings]. Is there any reason we couldn't put that money back in your pocket starting next month?"
