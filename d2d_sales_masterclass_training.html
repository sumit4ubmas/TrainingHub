The "non-sales" opening script

Why it works: you reference a local neighbor (social proof), ask for only 60 seconds (low commitment), and mention their pain — the dinner rush — before you even say what you sell.
Say this word for word "Hey there! I'm [Your Name], I'm just stopping by because I'm working with a few owners on this block — like [Mention a local neighbor] — to help them handle the dinner rush more efficiently. Is the owner or manager around for a quick 60 seconds, or are they buried in the kitchen right now?"

The "pattern interrupt" opener

Instead of "How are you today?" (which sounds like every salesperson ever), try this to instantly differentiate yourself:
Pattern interrupt — use at the door "I'll be brief — I know you're mid-shift."
The local drop — follow immediately with "I was just talking to the manager over at [Neighboring Restaurant] about their delivery tablet mess…"
These two lines do more work than a 2-minute pitch. They show respect for their time and prove you know their world.

Bad approach vs. great approach

❌ Bad approach
"Hi, I sell point-of-sale systems and wanted to see if you're interested in upgrading your current setup. Do you have a minute?"
Leads with the product. Owner mentally checks out instantly.
✅ Great approach
"I'll be brief — I know you're mid-shift. I was just at Tony's next door helping them fix their delivery tablet mess. Is the owner around for 60 seconds?"
Leads with respect + social proof. Sparks curiosity before pitching.

When the owner isn't there: gatekeeper strategy

Don't just leave a card — that goes in the trash. Use the gatekeeper to gather intel and book a specific return visit.
Ask the staff member "When is the 'calm before the storm' here? I want to make sure I don't catch them during a rush."
Always get a name "Who should I ask for? Marco? Great — I'll come back Thursday morning and ask for Marco."
Write the name down immediately. When you return and ask by name, you look like a trusted contact — not a cold caller.

Discovery: finding the "burn"

You can't solve a problem you haven't uncovered first

⏱ 7 min
The best reps listen 70%, talk 30%. Your questions should lead owners to realize their current system is costing them money — before you ever mention your product.

Four high-impact discovery questions

1
On efficiency — rush hour pain
"During a Friday night rush, what's the biggest bottleneck? Is it the kitchen getting orders late, or the line at the front?"
2
On delivery — tablet chaos
"How many tablets do you have buzzing over there? Do you have to manually re-type those DoorDash orders into your POS?"
3
On costs — hidden fees
"When was the last time your current provider sat down and showed you exactly how much you're losing in processing fees?"
4
The "magic wand" question
"If you could change one thing about how your staff takes orders today to make their lives easier, what would it be?"

The pitch: the value bridge

Don't list features — link their pain to your benefit

⏱ 7 min
Never pitch a feature without anchoring it to a pain you discovered. A feature without context is just noise to a busy owner.

The 4-step value bridge

StepExample script
1 The hook
Acknowledge pain		 "You mentioned that re-keying delivery orders is a nightmare and it's eating up your cashier all night."
2 The bridge
Introduce solution		 "Our system pulls those orders directly into the kitchen. No tablets, no manual entry — everything flows in automatically."
3 The payoff
Show the result		 "This saves your staff 5 minutes per order and eliminates human error entirely — no more wrong orders to the kitchen."
4 The proof
Social proof		 "That's exactly why [Local Restaurant] on your block switched last month — they cut ticket errors by 80%."

Feature dump vs. value bridge

❌ Feature dump
"Our system has cloud reporting, tableside ordering, a loyalty program, delivery integration with all major platforms, and inventory management…"
Owner's brain shuts off. Too much, no context.
✅ Value bridge
"You said those 3 tablets are driving you crazy. We consolidate all of that into one screen. No tablets, no retyping, no errors."
One problem, one solution, one outcome. Crystal clear.

Closing techniques: securing the win

"I'll think about it" usually means no — use these closes

⏱ 7 min
In D2D, hesitation kills deals. Every technique below creates a low-friction path to yes. Match the close to the conversation.

Close A — the assumptive close

Don't ask if they want it. Ask how they want it. Skip the yes/no gate entirely.
Script"Would it be easier for your staff to start with the handheld terminals for tableside ordering, or do you want to keep it strictly at the counter for now?"
❌ Don't say
"So… would you like to move forward with this?"
Opens the door for a "no."
✅ Do say
"Which setup makes more sense for your flow — handheld or counter-only to start?"
Both options move forward.

Close B — the savings close (the math win)

Put real numbers on paper. Owners respond to dollars, not features.
Script"Based on your volume, I'm confident we can wipe out about $[Amount] in monthly fees. If I can show you that on paper right now, is there any reason we couldn't get the paperwork started today?"
Always do the math in front of them — on a notepad or your phone calculator. Making it visual makes it real.

Close C — the implementation close

Use scarcity honestly. A real technician visit creates legitimate urgency.
Script"I have a technician in this zip code on Tuesday doing an install nearby. If I can get you on that schedule, we can have you up and running before the weekend rush. Does Tuesday morning work?"

Handling objections: the ACE framework

Every objection is a question in disguise — pause, then ACE it

⏱ 10 min

The 3-second rule

When a merchant raises an objection, wait 3 seconds before speaking. It shows you are listening — not just waiting to pitch. This single habit separates top reps from average ones.

The ACE framework

A
Acknowledge
Validate their concern so they don't feel attacked or dismissed
C
Clarify
Ask a question to find the real objection underneath the stated one
E
Explore
Present Plum POS as the bridge between their current pain and their goal

Real-world objections & response scripts

Objection 1
"I'm too busy right now. Come back later." Reality: they're busy AND they want you to leave
"I completely respect that — you've got a line out the door! I'm actually here because Plum POS helps restaurants like yours cut transaction times by 20% so that line moves faster. I'll be back Tuesday at 10 AM when it's quiet. Just so I'm prepared — are you more worried about your front-of-house speed or kitchen accuracy?"
ACE in action: Acknowledge the rush → Clarify where pain is worst → Explore with a specific return time.
Objection 2
"I'm happy with my current system." Reality: they haven't thought about what it's costing them
"I totally understand how you feel. A lot of owners I talk to felt the same way — until they found out they were paying 3% in hidden fees that we cut in half. Can I show you a quick comparison? Two minutes."
Uses the feel-felt-found method to create curiosity without confrontation.
Objection 3
"I already have Square / Clover / Toast." Reality: they fear the hassle of switching more than they like their current system
"Those are big names for a reason! Most owners I talk to use them. Usually, they stay because they think switching is a nightmare. If I could show you how our white-glove team handles the entire menu upload and hardware setup in one afternoon — while saving you $[Amount] in fees — would that be worth a 5-minute look?"
Validate their choice, then reframe switching as effortless rather than attacking their current system.
Objection 4
"I don't like contracts / The price is too high." Reality: they've been burned by hidden fees or "teaser rates" before
"I hear you. Nothing is worse than a 'teaser rate' that doubles in six months. Plum POS is built on transparency — we don't just sell hardware, we provide a growth tool. If the system pays for itself in labor savings within the first 60 days, would the upfront cost still be your main concern?"
Address the real fear (hidden fees), then reframe cost as an investment with a 60-day payback.
Objection 5
"I just signed a contract with my current provider."
"Totally fair — when does that contract come up? I'd love to circle back 30 days before so you have options. Mind if I grab your info? I won't contact you until then."
Don't force it — plant the seed, get contact info, and set a future trigger date.
Objection 6
"Send me some info."
"Of course — what's the best email? And honestly, the numbers are personalized to your volume. Could I get 5 minutes this Thursday to walk you through it? That way it actually means something."
"Send me info" is almost always a soft dismissal. Acknowledge, then anchor a specific follow-up time.

Pro tips & rep mindset

The habits that separate top earners from the pack

⏱ 4 min

Daily habits for top reps

🎯

The "No" challenge

Tell new reps to try and collect 50 "No's" in their first two days. It desensitizes them to rejection and leads them to "Yes" much faster.

🌅

Win the morning

Spend the first 30 minutes of every day role-playing the hardest objection from the day before. Repetition builds instinct.

💸

Visualize the commission

One "Yes" on a Plum POS system doesn't just earn a commission — it changes a local business owner's life. Remember that at every door.

📋

Review the ACE framework

Before each block, review your objection scripts. The reps who sound natural practiced the most — not the ones who winged it.

The mindset: owners are heroes

Every restaurant owner is a hero in their community. They employ local people, feed neighborhoods, and work 60-hour weeks. You are not there to sell them something — you are there to give them their time and money back. Carry that energy into every single door.
